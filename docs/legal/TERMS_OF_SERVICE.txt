1. WHAT NEXA IS

Nexa is a chat service. Your messages, files, and calls are encrypted
on your device with keys the server never has. Nobody who runs Nexa
can read them, monitor them, or switch that off. That is how the
software works, not a promise that could be quietly waived later. The
one way around it is an app that you or a server's manager choose to
add: what is meant for an app reaches it (section 3).

The owner and a team of moderators (called staff in these Terms) keep
Nexa running. Staff only ever work with the small part of Nexa that is
not encrypted: public profiles, what servers show everyone (their
names, icons, banners, and descriptions), community membership,
moderation cases, bug reports, and the delivery details the server
needs to work.

3. ENCRYPTION AND PRIVACY

- Message text, file contents and names, notes, call audio and
  video, and your settings are encrypted before they leave your
  device. The server stores only ciphertext it cannot read.
- Nobody who runs Nexa holds a key to any of it, apart from the keys
  of the apps people add (below). Other than adding an app, there is
  no admin panel, setting, or software switch that decrypts content
  already sent, and nothing in the software that intercepts future
  content in readable form.
- A few things are not end-to-end encrypted, because Nexa itself
  has to read them: your public profile, moderation cases and the
  threads that go with them, announcements from Nexa, bug reports
  you send, safety reports and the evidence you choose to share,
  messages that programs post through a server's webhooks,
  and what apps post and receive. The Privacy Policy explains each
  of them.
- You can report specific content and choose text, screenshots or recordings
  to share with Nexa staff. This creates a separate readable report; it does
  not give staff your conversation keys or access to other private content.
  Staff may remove a reported message or take other moderation actions.
- Apps are programs other people make in Settings, Developer Portal.
  Anyone can add one to their account, or to a server they manage.
  What reaches an app is not end-to-end encrypted from it: messages
  meant for it (the sender's app seals a copy for it, which the
  server opens to hand over), what you send it with its commands and
  forms, and, for an app made to read every message, every message in
  the channels it can see. An app shows what it can read before
  anyone adds it, and the start of each channel where one reads every
  message names it. Whoever made an app runs it and decides what it
  does with what it gets. Nexa does not check apps, so add only ones
  you trust.
- If you make an app, you are responsible for what it does, and these
  Terms apply to it as they do to you.
- The Privacy Policy lists exactly what is stored and who can see it.

5. HOW MODERATION WORKS

What staff can see is limited by the encryption. They moderate what is
public (profiles, what servers show everyone, and public images) and
what is reported to them. If someone you talk to shows staff something
you sent them, staff can act on it. Nobody goes looking through chats
or inside servers, because nobody here can.

When a rule is broken, staff can:

- Remove a profile picture or banner.
- Reset profile text. The username, display name, about me, or
  pronouns are replaced with MODERATOR_REMOVED_ followed by a random
  code, and a custom status is cleared. A reset username becomes
  moderator_removed_ followed by a random code, because usernames are
  lowercase. Your old username keeps signing you in, and nobody else
  can register it.
- Clear the nicknames an account uses in its servers.
- Take down parts of what a server shows everyone: its icon, banner,
  name, or description. Pictures are removed, the name is replaced
  with MODERATOR_REMOVED_ followed by a random code, and the
  description is cleared. The server's owner gets the notice and the
  case, and can set new ones that follow the rules. Staff never see
  the channels, messages, or files inside a server.
- Mute an account for a set time. A muted account can still read
  everything, but cannot send messages, react, join calls, or change
  its profile until the mute ends.
- Ban an account, for a set time or permanently. A banned account
  sees only the ban screen and its moderation cases, where it can
  appeal. Nothing in the account is removed while it is banned.
- Delete an account. This permanently removes the account and
  everything it posted and uploaded, including servers and group
  chats it owns. See section 7.
- In an emergency, such as an account someone else has taken over,
  switch off sign-in for an account without a notice.

Every action is taken for a reason, and you are told:

- You get a notice from Nexa, both as a message and as a pop-up,
  with the reason and the name of the member of staff who acted.
- Each notice opens a private case thread with the moderation team,
  where you can ask questions or appeal, and send pictures or videos.
  Staff can lift a mute or a ban early.
- When staff close a case, it stays readable, but nobody can write in
  it until staff open it again.
- Every staff action is recorded in an append-only log that cannot be
  edited or deleted, so what was done, by whom, and why stays on
  record.

Everything else only comes up if there is a valid legal order that
has to be followed. Even then, only what the server actually holds
can be handed over (see the Privacy Policy). Message text, file
contents, and call audio still cannot be handed over, because nobody
here has a way to read them, apart from what was opened for an app.
