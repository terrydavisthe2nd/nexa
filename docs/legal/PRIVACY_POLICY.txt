NEXA - PRIVACY POLICY
Site: nexa.fish (also at mathtest.lol, and formerly chat.evilion.xyz)
Contact the owner (nora) here: evilionx3@proton.me
Effective: 2026-11-02

This policy lists exactly what Nexa holds. It is written against the
actual database, so if something is not listed here, there is no place
for it and nothing to hand over. It also names every outside service
Nexa uses and what each one gets (section 6), and explains your rights
under data protection law, including the GDPR (sections 9 to 11).

1. THE SHORT VERSION

- Your messages, files, notes, and calls are encrypted end to end.
  Nobody who runs Nexa can read them, and nobody monitors what you do
  in a chat. The exceptions are messages that programs post through a
  server's webhooks, and apps: what an app posts, and what reaches an
  app that you or a server's manager added (section 3j). The app marks
  both.
- What the owner and staff can see is your public profile, what
  servers show everyone, public images, the delivery details the
  server needs (who sent what, where, and when), bug reports you send,
  and moderation records: the actions taken on an account or a server,
  and the case threads with Nexa that go with them.
- For security, Nexa keeps the IP addresses each account signs in and
  is active from (section 3e). Staff use them only to find accounts
  that share an address, such as a second account made to get around
  a ban. The moderation panel never shows the addresses themselves.
- Nexa uses a few outside services, listed in section 6 with what
  each one gets. Cloudflare carries all traffic to and from Nexa, runs
  the captcha, and relays calls. None of them can read your messages.
- Links you share are previewed by the server through Cloudflare, so
  the sites never see your address, and YouTube videos and music from
  Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer and SoundCloud play through the server,
  so those services never learn who is watching or listening.
- No email, no phone number, no real name, no payment details, no
  location, no contacts, no analytics, and no push tokens are ever
  collected. If you buy a plan, you pay on Stripe's page, and Nexa
  learns only which plan you have (section 3k).
- You can export your data, correct it, or delete your account
  yourself, and you can object or complain (section 11).

2. WHAT IS ENCRYPTED (NOBODY WHO RUNS NEXA CAN READ IT)

- Message text and edits. Stored only as ciphertext. The exceptions
  are a message a program posts through a server's webhook, or an app
  posts: it reaches the server readable and is stored as it came
  (sections 3d and 3j), and the app marks it as not end-to-end
  encrypted. A message meant for an app, and in some channels every
  message, is also opened for the app (section 3j).
- File contents, file names, file types, dimensions, and per file
  keys (the key travels inside the encrypted message). The files of a
  message opened for an app are opened for it too.
- Call audio and video. Calls are not recorded and are never written
  to disk.
- Notes, with their earlier versions and their files, and the note
  you can add to a friend request.
- Your settings (themes, notification choices, mutes, sound packs,
  message drafts, and recent searches), and the recordings in your
  sound packs. Only the size, the version number, and the last
  written time of your settings, and the size of each recording, can
  be seen.
- Your password and recovery key. Your password never leaves your
  device; your browser or app only sends a derived check value. Your
  recovery key is shown once on your device and never stored anywhere.
- Your private identity key and your conversation keys. Your private
  key sits on the server only wrapped by your password, by your
  recovery key, or by a passkey you added (wrapped with a secret only
  that passkey can produce, which never reaches the server).
  Conversation keys sit on the server only sealed separately to
  each member. Nobody who runs Nexa holds a key that can open any of
  this, except the keys of apps (section 3j). There is no spare key,
  no master key, and no escrow.

Apart from the apps people choose to add (section 3j), there is no
setting or admin action that decrypts content already sent, and
nothing in the software that hands over future content in readable
form.

3. WHAT THE OWNER AND STAFF CAN SEE

a) Profile information (public by design, typed by you): username,
   display name, profile picture and banner, about me, pronouns,
   accent colors, custom status and expiry, how your typing indicator
   looks, the server tag you wear, your profile widgets (the games and
   music you show, and the cards you make), presence preference, DM
   privacy setting, whether friends can add you to group chats, per
   server nicknames and pictures, account ID, creation and last change
   times, admin and disabled flags, and your plan (Supporter or Pro,
   shown as a badge). None of this is identity
   checked. Servers are public in the same way: a server's name, icon,
   banner, description, and tag, its owner, and when it was made.
   Staff see these with how many members and channels a server has
   (the numbers, not the channels), but never what is said inside it.

b) Public images (stored readable, can be handed over if ordered):
   profile pictures, banners, server icons and banners, group chat
   pictures, server tag badges, custom emoji and stickers (with their
   names, and the names and descriptions of their packs), the pictures
   on profile widgets and typing indicators, and webhook pictures.

c) Delivery details (needed to send and notify, not content): message
   ID, channel, author, type, timestamps, edit time, reply target,
   mentions (user, role, and everyone flags are kept outside the
   ciphertext so the server knows who to notify), file presence and
   sizes, reactions (who reacted, with which emoji), pins, read
   positions, poll vote positions (not the question or option text,
   which is encrypted), and the line a call leaves in a direct message
   or group chat, which shows who started the call and when (whether
   anyone answered, and for how long, is encrypted inside it).

d) Membership and structure (typed by users, stored as typed):
   friends, requests, and blocks, the private nicknames you give your
   friends, server memberships and roles, DM and group participants,
   channel and group chat names, topics, and structure, invite codes
   you made (uses and expiry, but not who used them; when signing up
   needs an invite, the one each account signed up with is noted),
   bans, per community audit log entries, who can read and write each
   note, invitations to notes, when each version of a note was saved,
   and a server's webhooks (their names, pictures, and who made them).
   A message a webhook posts is stored readable, as it arrived, with
   any files that came with it.

e) Sessions, account addresses and guest entries (the only places an
   IP address is written): one entry per signed in device, with the
   session ID, the IP address it was last used from (kept up to date
   while you use it), the browser or app description, and the sign in
   and last active times. You can see your own, and sign any of them
   out, in Settings → Devices. Staff see when an account was last
   active in the moderation panel. Entries are deleted on sign out or
   after 90 days without use. A locked subchat also notes which
   sessions typed its password (the password itself is not kept; it
   only unlocks the screen, and messages stay encrypted under the
   conversation key).
   - Account addresses: every IP address your
     account has signed in or been active from, with when it was first
     and last seen there. The list is kept up to date while you use
     Nexa and stays after you sign out. It is there for security: the
     moderation panel shows staff which other accounts have used the
     same addresses (marked "Alt Accounts", with how many addresses
     they share), so they can spot accounts made to get around a mute
     or a ban. The panel never shows the addresses themselves; the
     owner produces them only to answer a valid legal order (Terms
     section 5). Several people on one network (a home, a school, a
     VPN) can share an address, so staff treat a match as something to
     look into, not proof. An address is deleted 180 days after your
     account last used it, and all of them are deleted with your
     account.
   - Guest entries: someone who joins a call through a guest link gets
     an entry with the name they typed, the call, the IP address they
     joined from, and when they joined. Nobody sees that address in
     the app. The entry is deleted when they leave or are removed,
     when the link is revoked, after about two minutes without a
     connection, or 12 hours after they joined, whichever comes first.
   - In memory only: to limit how often an address can sign in,
     connect, and make other requests, the server counts requests by
     IP address in memory, never on disk, and forgets each address
     within a few hours of its last request. The captcha also sends
     your address to Cloudflare, or to hCaptcha from an older app
     (section 6).

f) Login checks (values not handed over, listed to close the point):
   the password check value, the password stretching salt and
   settings, your public key, your wrapped private key (cannot be
   opened without your password), and the wrapped recovery copy with
   its check value. For each passkey you add: its public key and id,
   the name you gave it, the site it was made for, when it was added
   and last used, a sign-in counter, and your private key wrapped by
   it (cannot be opened without that passkey). With two-factor sign
   in on, also its secret, sealed with a key only the server has (the
   server needs it to check your codes), and your backup codes as
   keyed hashes, with when each was used. None of these is a password,
   and none can be turned into one.

g) Moderation records (not end-to-end encrypted, kept as the record
   of how Nexa is moderated):
   - Moderation cases: which action was taken on an account or on a
     server (a server's case belongs to its owner), the reason, which
     member of staff took it, when, how long it lasts, and the values
     a reset replaced (for example the old username, or a server's
     old name and description).
   - Case threads: the messages, pictures, and videos between a member
     and staff in a moderation case, stored in readable form and
     readable by every member of staff. Only the member and staff can
     open the pictures and videos. A closed case keeps its thread.
   - Safety reports: who reported which item, the reason,
     description, the complete content and all files of a reported message,
     optional text, pictures or recordings supplied with other reports,
     and a snapshot of the reported item's readable details. These are not
     end-to-end encrypted. Reported files include their individual file keys
     so staff can read them. Reports do not share conversation or note keys,
     or grant staff access to the rest of an encrypted conversation. Staff

     close a report, they may also write the reporter a short message
     about the outcome; the reporter sees it, with the report's status,
     in their Nexa conversation and in Settings, but never the notes or
     who reviewed it. The reported person is not shown the reporter's
     identity or the report itself.
   - The moderation log: an append-only record of every staff action
     (who, to whom, what, why, and when), with the names as they were
     at the time. Nobody can edit or delete it.
   - Held usernames: a username reset by staff stays attached to its
     account, so it still signs that account in, and nobody else can
     register it.
   - Fingerprints of pictures staff removed, so the same picture
     cannot be put back.
   - Announcements from Nexa, with who wrote them.
   - Whether you have seen each notice, and how far you have read
     your messages from Nexa.

h) Day to day running: unfinished upload progress (expected and
   received sizes), which sticker packs you installed and which
   stickers you used last, crash reports from the web app (the account

   request logs (page, result, size, and duration, with no IP address
   recorded there).

i) Activity and a linked Discord account (optional): what the
   desktop app or a linked Discord account says you are doing, such
   as a game or a song, goes to the people who can see your status
   while it lasts and is kept in memory only, never written down. If
   you link a Discord account (Settings → Activity), Nexa keeps its
   Discord ID, username, display name, and picture, when you linked
   it, which kinds of activity you chose to show, whether it is in the
   small Discord server Nexa's bot reads activity in, and Discord's
   sign in tokens for it, sealed with a key only the server has, so
   the bot can add it back to that server. Unlinking deletes all of it
   at once.

j) Apps (not end-to-end encrypted, added only by people who choose
   to): an app is a program someone made in Settings, Developer
   Portal, with a bot account its maker runs. People add apps to their
   own account or to a server they manage. For each app Nexa keeps its
   name, description, pictures, tags, and links, the addresses it is
   sent interactions and events at, its settings, its commands, hashes
   of the token its bot signs in with and of its secret, its bot's own
   key (sealed with a key only the server has), where it was added (by
   whom, and when), who may use its commands in each server, and
   hashes of the sign in tokens it holds for accounts that let it in.
   What reaches an app is readable to it, and to the server on the way:
   - Messages an app posts are stored readable, as they came, as a
     webhook's are.
   - A message meant for an app (a direct message to its bot, a
     mention of it, a reply to it, or a message someone uses one of its
     commands on) carries a copy that the sender's app seals for the
     app's bot. The copy is kept with the message, and the server opens
     it, with the message's files, to hand them to the app.
   - An app its maker set to read every message holds the keys of the

     message in the channels it can see, earlier ones included, to hand

     of each of those channels names it.

     where, and when, for 15 minutes (files for a day), as are the

   - Like a member, an app's bot sees the delivery details (3c) and the
     members (3d) of the servers and channels it is in, and, if its
     maker turned it on, who is online and what they are doing.

k) Plans (only if you buy one or are given one; not end-to-end

l) Call usage (not end-to-end encrypted, amounts only): how much call
   traffic each account used each day (by UTC): the media sent to its

   to other people, the media relayed to it through Cloudflare's TURN
   service, and how long it was in calls, both as the server estimates
   them and as Cloudflare measures them. Staff (admins and up) see it

   allowance everyone shares each month. It says nothing about which
   calls you were in, who was in them, or what was said. To put
   Cloudflare's measurement to a name, the server also keeps which
   account opened each connection to the call service, by its random
   ID, with when it was opened, and the name of each relay credential
   it hands out (never the password half), so a credential can be
   revoked two minutes after its call ends. An account that relays
   more than the day's limit through TURN gets no relay until the next

4. WHAT IS NEVER COLLECTED

No email address. No phone number. No legal name, birth date, age, or
ID documents. No payment details (Stripe takes those, section 6). No IP addresses beyond the sessions,
account addresses and guest entries above. No access history beyond
when each of those addresses was first and last used. No online time
history. No call records beyond the line in 3c and the amounts in 3l:
who was in which call is held in memory while the call lasts and
otherwise exists only inside that line's encrypted summary. No device or
advertising IDs and no push tokens. No location. No address book
imports. No link clicks, analytics, or tracking of any kind. No record
of what you search for: message search runs on your device, using
messages it already decrypted, and GIF, game, and music searches pass
through the server to the services in section 6, with recent results
kept in memory (GIFs for two minutes, games and music for a day) and
nothing saying who searched. No readable drafts or unsent copies:
drafts live only inside your encrypted settings.

5. WHO SEES WHAT

  decrypts them, and the communities and channels you share with
  them.
- The owner and the staff they appoint see sections 3a to 3j, and
  admins and up see 3l. Staff use a moderation panel that shows
  public profiles, what servers show everyone, when accounts were last
  active, which accounts have used the same IP addresses (never the
  addresses), how much call traffic each account used, moderation
  cases and their threads, the log, announcements, bug reports and
  safety reports.
  For a reported message, staff can read its complete content and files,
  not the rest of the encrypted conversation.
- Link previews, pictures and videos linked from elsewhere, GIFs,
  YouTube videos, and music from Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer and
  SoundCloud are fetched by the Nexa server, not by your device,
  and the server fetches them through Cloudflare's WARP network. The
  sites see a Cloudflare address, never yours and never the server's,
  and your device never connects to them. A YouTube video plays in
  YouTube's own player, loaded through the server the same way, in a
  frame that cannot reach anything else in the app: YouTube is told
  the video is shown on the Nexa address you are using, as any site
  that shows one says, and gets your browser's description, but not
  who is watching, and it gets no cookies. Spotify's and SoundCloud's
  own players load through the server in the same kind of frame and
  are told nothing about the page they are shown on. Apple Music's and
  Deezer's thirty-second previews play in Nexa's own player, fetched
  by the server. Nothing ties a fetch to who
  asked for it, and a preview is kept in memory for at most an hour so
  the same link is not fetched twice. In a chat, a link is only
  previewed when you send it, and you can turn previews off in
  Settings → Chat.
- An app is run by whoever made it, not by Nexa. What reaches an app
  (section 3j) goes to its maker's program, and from there the app's
  own terms apply. An app with an address of its own is sent its
  interactions and events there by the server, through Cloudflare's
  WARP network. Nexa does not check apps, so add only ones you trust,
  and take one back at any time in Settings, Authorized Apps, or in a
  server's Integrations.
- A theme, plugin, or stylesheet you add from a link, and any fonts or
  stylesheets your own custom CSS loads, come straight from the site
  that hosts them: your device fetches them when you add them and
  again from time to time to keep them up to date, so that site sees
  your IP address, as it would if you visited it.
- Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with advertisers or
  analytics companies. The only outside services are the ones in
  section 6, and each gets only what its job needs. Beyond them,
  nothing is handed over except as forced by a valid legal order (see
  Terms section 5), and even then only what exists above. Encrypted
  content still cannot be handed over, apart from what was opened for
  an app (section 3j).

6. SERVICE PROVIDERS

These are the outside services Nexa uses. Each gets only what its job
needs, and none of them can read your messages, files, notes, or
calls.

- Stripe, for plans. When you buy a plan or a gift, your app opens
  Stripe's checkout page, and managing a plan opens Stripe's own page
  for it. Stripe sells the plan as the merchant of record, so it
  collects your payment details, name, email address and billing
  address, works out the tax, and keeps the records of your payments
  that the law requires of it. Nexa gets only what section 3k lists.
  Stripe's own privacy policy covers what Stripe keeps. Why: to take
  payments without Nexa ever holding card details.
- Cloudflare, for the network. Every connection to Nexa reaches the
  server through Cloudflare's network (a Cloudflare Tunnel), so
  Cloudflare handles your IP address and every request your device
  makes. Your messages, files, notes, and calls are already encrypted
  when they pass through it. Why: so the server is never reachable
  directly from the internet.
- Cloudflare Turnstile, for the captcha when you sign up, sign in,
  recover an account, send a friend request, invite someone to a
  note, create or join a server, or join a call as a guest. Your
  browser or app loads it from challenges.cloudflare.com, and the
  server sends Cloudflare your IP address with its answer to check
  it. Why: to stop bots from making accounts, guessing passwords, and
  sending spam.
- hCaptcha, for the same captcha in older versions of the apps that
  do not have Turnstile yet. Those load it from hcaptcha.com, and the
  server sends hCaptcha your IP address with its answer to check it.
  Why: so older apps keep working until they update.
- Cloudflare Realtime, for calls. It relays call audio and video, so
  your device connects to it and it sees your IP address, and the
  server labels your connection with your account ID when it sets the
  call up. Calls stay end-to-end encrypted, so Cloudflare cannot hear
  or see them. Why: so calls work between any two networks.
- Cloudflare WARP, for fetching things from other sites. Link
  previews, pictures and videos linked from elsewhere, GIFs, YouTube
  videos, and game and music searches go out through it, so
  Cloudflare sees which sites are contacted, and those sites see a
  Cloudflare address instead of the server's. Why: so the sites
  people link never learn where the server is.
- Klipy, for GIF search. The server sends it the words you search for
  (or asks for trending GIFs) and nothing about you: Klipy sees the
  server's address, not yours. The GIFs themselves load through the
  server and WARP, never from Klipy to your device.
- IGDB, for the games on profile widgets. The server sends it the
  words you search for and asks it about the games people show,
  through WARP, and keeps the game covers it shows.
- Deezer, for the music on profile widgets. The server sends it the
  words you search for and asks it about the songs, albums, and
  artists people show, through WARP. It keeps their pictures, and
  streams song previews through itself without keeping them.
- Telegram, for importing a Telegram sticker set. The server asks
  Telegram's Bot API for the set by its name and downloads its
  stickers. Telegram learns the set's name and nothing about you.
- Discord, only if you link a Discord account, or play games that
  report to Discord while the desktop app runs. Linking takes you to
  Discord to approve it, and then Discord knows your Discord account
  was linked to Nexa: Nexa's bot adds it to a small Discord server and
  reads your Discord presence there. The server also asks Discord,
  through WARP, for the public name and pictures of the games an
  activity names. On the desktop app, what games tell Discord passes
  through Nexa to Discord when Discord is running, as it would without
  Nexa. Discord's own privacy policy covers what Discord keeps.

7. HOW LONG THINGS ARE KEPT

- Sessions: until you sign out, or after 90 days without use.
- Guest entries: until the guest leaves or is removed, the link is
  revoked, about two minutes pass without a connection, or 12 hours
  after they joined, whichever comes first. Guest links: until they
  are revoked, or once they have expired and nobody who joined
  through them is left.
- Account addresses: 180 days after the account last used each one,
  or until the account is deleted.
- IP addresses counted in memory: a few hours after the last request.
- Messages and files: until they are deleted: by their author, by a
  conversation or server owner, with the author's account, or, in a
  direct message, with the account of the person on the other side
  when they delete it themselves.
- Notes, with their earlier versions: until their owner deletes them
  or their owner's account.
- Unfinished uploads, and files nothing points at any more: removed
  after a day.
- Link previews: in memory for up to an hour. GIF search results: in
  memory for two minutes. Game and music search results: in memory
  for a day.
- Crash reports: only about the newest 500 are kept.
- Bug reports and their threads: until your account is deleted.
- Plans (3k), with the gifts you were given: while your account
  exists, including after a plan or gift ends.
- Call usage (3l): about two months (days older than 62 are deleted).
  Which account opened each call connection: three days. Relay
  credential names: until the credential expires, within an hour.
- A linked Discord account: until you unlink it or delete your
  account.
- Apps: until their maker deletes them or the maker's account. What
  you send an app, and its replies only you see: 15 minutes (files
  sent to an app: a day). The codes an app trades for sign in tokens:
  10 minutes. An app's sign in tokens: until you take the app back,
  or after 90 days without use.
- Moderation cases, case threads, held usernames, fingerprints of
  removed pictures, safety reports with their snapshots, evidence and review
  history, and the moderation log: kept as the moderation
  record, including after an account is deleted.
- Backups: a copy of everything the server stores, made from time to
  time (usually before an update) and kept on the same machine.
  Making a new backup deletes the one before, so only the newest
  exists, and something deleted from Nexa stays in it until the next
  backup replaces it. It holds the same encrypted content as the
  server, under the same encryption.

8. DELETION

- Deleted messages, files, and accounts are removed from the
  database. No trash bin or archive is kept; only the newest backup
  (section 7) holds a copy until the next one replaces it.
- Deleting your own account deletes everything it made: your messages
  everywhere, the files you uploaded, your reactions, votes, and
  packs, the notes you own, your bug reports and sound recordings, the
  invite and guest links you made, the servers and group chats you own
  (for everyone in them), the apps you made with their bots, and your
  direct message conversations, for both people in them. Its profile
  is overwritten, and its sessions, account addresses, relationships,
  settings, keys, second factor, linked Discord account, crash
  reports, call usage, plan records and the gifts given to it are
  deleted, and a
  running subscription is cancelled at once. A gift it gave someone
  else stays theirs until it ends, and a gift link it bought can still
  be claimed; one it claimed no longer says who claimed it. Stripe
  keeps its own record of past payments. What is left is a stand-in
  named Deleted User, with the account ID, when it was made, its
  public key, and its color and privacy choices, which nobody can
  sign in as.
- When someone you have a direct message conversation with deletes
  their own account, that conversation is deleted for you too.
- When staff delete an account, everything it posted and uploaded is
  removed with it, everywhere, servers and group chats it owns are
  deleted for everyone in them, and so are the apps it made, with
  their bots. Its direct message conversations stay for the people on
  the other side, without its messages. The moderation case, its
  thread, and the log entry stay.

Under the GDPR you have these rights, and this is how to use them:

- Access and portability: Settings → Your Data → Export My Data
  downloads your profile, settings, friends, servers, channels, and
  every message you hold keys for, decrypted on your device, as one
  file you can take elsewhere. Settings → Devices lists your sign ins
  with their IP addresses. For anything the export leaves out, such
  as your moderation cases or bug reports, contact the owner.
- Correction: change your profile at any time in Settings. For
  anything you cannot change yourself, contact the owner.
- Erasure: delete your messages and files yourself, or your whole
  account in Settings → My Account → Delete Account (section 8 says
  what that removes). Moderation records stay, for the reasons in
  section 10.
- Restriction and objection: you can ask for your data to be used
  less while something is being checked, and object to any use based

  either.
- Changing your mind: unlink Discord in Settings → Activity, turn
  link previews off in Settings → Chat, or remove a theme or plugin
  you added, at any time.
- Complaints: you can complain to the data protection authority where
  you live or work, or where you think the problem happened.
